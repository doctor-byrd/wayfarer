# Trade & Economy System

#### 1. The Core Concept: Supply, Demand, and Logistics
Wealth in Dantor isn’t just about finding gold in a dungeon; it’s about moving the right goods to the right place. However, goods have **Burden**. You cannot carry a fortune in your pockets. Meaningful trade requires planning, pack animals, carts, or ships, making logistics a core part of the adventure.

#### 2. Trade Good Categories
Trade goods are abstracted into "Units" (e.g., a cartload of grain, a chest of fine silk, a crate of rare herbs). Each Unit has a **Base Price** (in Crowns) and a **Burden** value.

| Category | Examples | Base Price (per Unit) | Burden |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Provisions** | Grain, salt, basic tools, rope, common herbs. | 1–5 Crowns | 1 |
| **Crafted** | Weapons, armor, fine clothing, wagons, quality gear. | 10–50 Crowns | 2 |
| **Rare / Luxury** | Kirksteel ingots, River-glass, exotic spices, fine art, ancient tomes. | 100–500 Crowns | 1 |
| **Contraband** | Stolen goods, forbidden magic, smuggled weapons, illicit poisons. | 50–200 Crowns | 1 |

#### 3. Settlement Tags (The Market)
Every town, city, or region has one or more **Tags** that dictate its economy. These tags modify the Buy and Sell prices of specific Trade Good categories.

| Settlement Tag | Description | Price Modifiers |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Agrarian** | Farming villages, breadbasket regions. | **Buy/Sell Provisions:** Cheap to buy, poor sell price. <br>**Buy Crafted/Rare:** Expensive. |
| **Metropolis** | Capital cities, major trade hubs, wealthy ports. | **Buy/Sell Crafted/Rare:** Excellent sell prices, high demand. <br>**Buy Provisions:** Expensive (must be imported). |
| **Frontier** | Remote settlements, mining camps, border towns. | **Buy/Sell Crafted:** High demand, good sell price. <br>**Buy Rare:** Very expensive. |
| **Arcane / Academic** | Centers of learning, magical enclaves, libraries. | **Buy/Sell Rare:** Exceptional demand for lore and magical components. |

*(GM Rule of Thumb: A "Good" match between a good and a tag means the player can sell it for **+50%** of Base Price, or buy it for **-25%**. A "Bad" match means selling for **-50%** or buying for **+50%**.)*

#### 4. The Commerce Check (The Mechanic)
When players attempt to buy or sell goods in bulk, or negotiate a special contract, they make a **Paired Roll**.
*   **Facet:** **Wit** (for appraising, spotting fakes, understanding markets) or **Resolve** (for steadfast haggling).
*   **Craft:** **Haggling**, **Appraisal**, or **Local Customs** (often provided by Jobs like Courier, Envoy, or Tinker).
*   **Target Number:** **TN 7** (Routine market), **TN 9** (Foreign or heavily regulated market), **TN 11** (Black market or hostile territory).

**Outcomes:**
*   **Success:** You secure the modified price (buying cheap or selling dear). 
*   **High Crit (Boon):** Exceptional profit. You find a wealthy patron willing to pay double, or you uncover a hidden stash of goods to buy at a steep discount. Gain 1 Milestone if this profit funds a major party goal.
*   **Failure:** You get the base price, or the deal falls through. Time is wasted (adding to the Adventure’s daily cycle).
*   **Fumble (Bane):** Catastrophic market failure. You are scammed (bought fake goods), the local guild blacklists you, or your conspicuous wealth attracts the attention of **Bandits** or **Corrupt Guards** (triggering an immediate Encounter).

#### 5. Logistics & Upkeep
*   **Burden Limits:** A single character can carry a personal Burden equal to their **Might die value × 2** (e.g., Might d8 = 16 Burden) before suffering movement penalties. 
*   **Transport:** To move meaningful wealth, the party needs a **Pack Animal** (+20 Burden, costs 5 Crowns/week to feed), a **Cart/Wagon** (+50 Burden, requires a beast to pull), or a **Ship** (hundreds of Burden, requires a crew). The *Beast-keeper*’s *Pack Bond* Talent is invaluable here.
*   **Lifestyle Upkeep:** Between Adventures, characters must pay a modest upkeep (e.g., 5–10 Crowns) for lodging, food, and gear maintenance. Failing to pay forces the character to take on debt or a temporary "Hardship" (e.g., −1 step to all social checks in that city until the debt is paid).

#### 6. The Risk of the Road (Tying Trade to Combat/Parley)
Carrying valuable goods is a beacon for trouble. When traveling with **Rare** or **Contraband** goods, the GM should increase the frequency of Encounters. 
*   *Bandits* will have the **Desire**: "Your cargo."
*   *City Guards* might have the **Desire**: "A bribe to look the other way" (perfect for the Envoy’s *Silver Tongue* or a Courier’s *Haggling*).
