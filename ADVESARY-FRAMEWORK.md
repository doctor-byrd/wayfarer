# Adversary Framework

#### Track 1: Character-Built Adversaries (Rivals, Bosses, Major Factions)
**When to use:** Recurring villains, faction leaders, rival Wayfarers, or any NPC with a name, a face, and an ongoing role in the story.
**How to build:** They are built **exactly like a Player Character**. They have:
*   4 Facets (Might, Grace, Wit, Resolve)
*   A Discipline and a **Job** (they can use standard PC Jobs, or special **Antagonist Jobs**, see below).
*   Vitality, Wounds, and an Aura pool.
*   A Heart (Desire and Fear).
*   Talents and Rites.

*Why this is amazing:* When the party finally corners the Crimson Captain, the GM doesn't look at a generic monster stat block. They look at the Captain’s sheet: *"Ah, the Captain is Battered, so they have a -1 step penalty. They have 2 Aura left. Their Fear is 'humiliation.' If the Envoy taunts them about their disgraced past, I can trigger a Spark and force a dramatic mistake."*

#### Track 2: The Framework Adversaries (Mooks, Monsters, Minions)
**When to use:** Wild beasts, generic city guards, undead skeletons, spirits, or nameless thugs.
**How to build:** The streamlined **Vitality / Strike / Evasion / Traits** block we discussed previously. They do not have Facets, Aura, or a complex Heart. They are obstacles, not characters.

### Introducing: Antagonist Jobs (NPC-Only)
To make Character-Built Adversaries feel uniquely threatening, you can give them **Antagonist Jobs**. These are custom Jobs designed specifically for villains and rivals. They follow the exact same rules as PC Jobs (Trained Crafts, 2 Talents, Kit) but are tuned for opposition.

Here are three examples:

#### 1. The Warlord *(Antagonist Job)*
A tactical commander who leads from the front and turns the battlefield to their advantage.
*   **Trained Crafts:** Command, heavy weapons, intimidation.
*   **Talents:** 
    *   *Rally the Troops:* Once per scene, when an allied Nuisance or Standard enemy is defeated, the Warlord gains 1 Aura and all remaining allies gain +1 step on their next attack.
    *   *Unyielding Presence:* The Warlord cannot be forced to move or Knocked Down by non-magical means.
*   **Kit:** Heavy weapon (poleaxe or greatsword), imposing armor, a banner or sigil of their faction.

#### 2. The Shadow *(Antagonist Job)*
An assassin, spy, or elusive rival who strikes from the dark and vanishes before retaliation.
*   **Trained Crafts:** Stealth, poisons, acrobatics.
*   **Talents:**
    *   *Elusive:* The first time the Shadow is targeted by an attack in a scene, they may spend 0 Aura to automatically add +2 steps to their Evasion against that attack.
    *   *Lethal Precision:* When attacking a target that is Battered or Down, the Shadow’s Strike die steps up (e.g., d6 becomes d8).
*   **Kit:** Dual daggers or a shortbow, darkened cloak, vials of poison or smoke pellets.

#### 3. The Corruptor *(Antagonist Job)*
A magic user who has twisted one of the Five Truths for selfish or destructive ends.
*   **Trained Crafts:** Rites (any one Pillar, but twisted), deception, lore.
*   **Talents:**
    *   *Twisted Rite:* When the Corruptor casts a Rite, they may choose to inflict 1 Wound on themselves to automatically treat the Paired Roll as a High Crit (Boon), but the Rite gains a horrific, dangerous side-effect determined by the GM.
    *   *Siphon Spirit:* When the Corruptor reduces a PC to 0 Vitality, the Corruptor immediately regains 1d4 Aura.
*   **Kit:** A corrupted focus (e.g., a blackened staff, a vial of tainted blood), dark robes, a journal of forbidden experiments.

### How This Looks in Practice

**Example A: A Recurring Rival (Track 1)**
> **Valeria, The Disgraced Knight** *(Adventure Level 2)*
> - **Facets:** Might d8, Grace d8, Wit d6, Resolve d8
> - **Discipline:** Vanguard | **Job:** Spellblade (Ember)
> - **Vitality:** 28 | **Wounds:** 0 | **Aura:** 4/4
> - **Heart:** **Desire:** "To clear her name and reclaim her family's estate." **Fear:** "Being seen as a monster by the people she tries to protect."
> - **Talents:** Arcane Strike, Mana Shield.
> *GM Note:* Valeria is a rival, not a monster. The party can Parley with her. If they appeal to her Desire (helping her clear her name), she might lower her sword. If they attack her, she fights tactically, spending Aura to Mana Shield and using Arcane Strike.

**Example B: A Monster Encounter (Track 2)**
> **Cave Stalker** *(Standard Framework)*
> - **Vitality:** 25 | **Evasion:** 11 | **Armor:** 1
> - **Strike:** d8 + 2 (Rending Claws)
> - **Desire:** "Fresh meat."
> - **Fear:** "Bright light or fire."
> - **Traits:** *Ambusher* (free action on surprise), *Pack Tactics* (+1 step if allied with another Stalker).
